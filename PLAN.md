# Part 1: Product (approve this)

## In one line

**Hermen helps you get ready for therapy.** You talk it through first, in private.
Then in the session you can say what you mean, and check that your therapist
heard what you meant.

Tagline: **Get ready to be understood.**

## The problem

Therapy is hard to start: cost, finding someone, stigma, time. One reason gets
missed: **being misunderstood**, and the fear of it.

**Hermeneutics, in plain words:** the study of how people understand each other.
It is about the gap between what one person says and what the other person hears.
The word comes from Hermes, the Greek messenger god who carried words between gods
and humans. Every message went through an interpreter, and something could get lost
on the way.

Therapy is where that gap hurts most:

- You arrive vulnerable, to sit with a stranger.
- You are on a clock: about 50 minutes.
- You are trying to say things you may never have said out loud, even to yourself.
- When we are vulnerable, we lose our words. We say something close to what we
  mean, but not quite it.
- The therapist interprets through their own lens. If neither of you notices the
  mismatch (and usually neither does), the session heads the wrong way. Sometimes
  for weeks.

> Example: *"I'm just tired of everything."* Burned out at work? Done with a
> relationship? Something darker? Each reading leads to a very different session,
> and you may never learn which one your therapist heard.

What helps is doing the work first: putting it into words before the session,
trying the same thing a few different ways, and checking whether you were understood.

> "Before I went to therapy, I did a lot of work on voicing what was going on with
> me. It had a great impact on how I could talk with my therapist. I would even
> phrase the same thing in different ways, to see if my therapist was understanding
> me." (founder)

That work is very hard, and nobody should have to do it alone. But sometimes we
can't say these things to the people around us yet, or even to ourselves.

**That's what Hermen is for.** Talking to an AI *instead of* therapy isn't the
answer. Preparing with an AI *before* real therapy is.

## What Hermen does: one session, about 10 minutes

1. **Pick your guide: Hermen or Herma.** No account needed to start. (Sign in with
   Masky, as an avatar with no real name or email, only to save your card.)
2. **Say it messy.** "What's been going on? However it comes out is fine." One
   gentle question at a time. No rush, no judgment.
3. **"Here's what I heard."** Hermen reflects back what it understood, then flags
   where your words could be read two ways: *"When you say 'tired of everything',
   a therapist might hear burnout, or might hear hopelessness. Which is closer?"*
   You correct it. **Say → reflect → correct** is the core loop.
4. **Say it three ways.** For the hardest thing, Hermen helps you write three
   versions: plain, gentle, and with an example. If the first one doesn't land in
   session, you have another way to say it.
5. **Take your prep card.** One page to bring to the session (print it, copy it, or
   keep it on your phone):
   - What I most want my therapist to understand
   - The hardest thing, three ways
   - Words I use that might be misread, and what I mean by them
   - What I want from this session (vent / advice / a plan / just be heard)
   - A check-back question for the therapist: *"Can you tell me what you heard?"*

## Meet Hermen and Herma

Same brain, same rules, different face and voice. You pick who you would rather
open up to.

| | **Hermen** | **Herma** |
|---|---|---|
| Name | From *Hermes* and *hermeneutics*: the messenger, the interpreter. | A *herma* was a stone marker at Greek crossroads and doorways, a guardian of thresholds. She waits with you outside the therapy door. |
| Look | Man, about 40, Mediterranean features. Kind, slightly tired eyes, a small real smile. Short dark hair, grey at the temples, short beard. Oatmeal knit sweater. Small silver wing pin (a nod to Hermes). Warm late-afternoon light, plain warm background. | Woman, about 40, Mediterranean features. Dark curly hair loosely tied back, a few strands loose. Warm, attentive face, small smile. Sage-green cardigan, small gold hoops, the same silver wing pin. Same light and background, so they read as a pair. |
| Voice | Low-medium, calm, unhurried. | Mid, warm, clear, unhurried. |
| Feels like | A friend who listens well. Not a doctor. | The same. |

**How they behave:** plain words, short replies, one question at a time. They use
your words, not jargon. They say "I might have this wrong" and mean it. They never
diagnose, never give medical advice, never say "I know exactly how you feel." They
always point you back to your human therapist.

Each one greets you with a short talking video on the landing page (made with Masky):

- **Hermen:** "Hi, I'm Hermen. Before your next session, let's find the words
  together. Messy is fine. Then we'll check that what you say is what you mean."
- **Herma:** "Hi, I'm Herma. Therapy goes better when you've already found some of
  the words. Let's practice, so you walk in ready to be understood."

## Safety and privacy (non-negotiable)

- On every screen: **"Hermen is not a therapist and is not for emergencies."**
- If you mention suicide, self-harm, or being in danger, Hermen stops the exercise,
  says why, kindly, and shows crisis lines: **988** (US), **116 123** Samaritans
  (UK and Ireland), **findahelpline.com** (anywhere), or your local emergency
  number. This is hard-coded in the page; the AI does not get to decide it.
- **We don't save your conversation.** It lives in your browser tab. Close the tab
  and it's gone. Your words go to the AI model only to write the replies.
- **Saving is your choice.** Signed in, you can save a prep card and come back to it
  before your session. Only the card is saved (never the chat), under your Masky
  avatar, and you can delete it anytime.
- You sign in as a Masky avatar, so we never learn your real name or email.

## The 60-second demo

1. Landing page: Hermen and Herma side by side, each with a talking intro.
2. Pick Hermen or Herma (each card plays a short hello on click).
3. Type something messy: *"idk, I've been tired of everything lately and my sister
   says I'm being dramatic."*
4. Hermen reflects it back, flags "tired of everything" as easy to misread, and
   asks which reading is closer.
5. "Say it three ways", then "Make my prep card". A one-page card appears. Print it,
   or sign in with Masky and save it.

## Not in v1 (cut for time)

Saved history, accounts beyond sign-in, therapist-side features, voice input,
booking, other languages, mobile app.
Stretch, only if time remains: Hermen/Herma **read your card aloud** (Masky voice),
and a **tip button** (Masky credits).

## Stack

- TanStack Start (React 19, SSR) on Cloudflare Workers, custom domain hermeneutic.live.
  Scaffolded with the TanStack CLI (exact command in AGENTS.md).
- TanStack Router (file routes), Query (server state), Form (intake), Store (prep state),
  DB (saved-cards collection), AI (chat stream + structured prep card).
- Anthropic `claude-sonnet-5-5` through `@tanstack/ai-anthropic`.
- Prisma 7 on Postgres, with a per-request client (Workers can't share connections).
- Login with Masky (OAuth code flow, HMAC-signed session cookie). Chat works signed
  out; saving a card needs sign-in.
- OpenShaders theme (dark-first), with a generated WebGL shader card per persona.
- GitHub Actions deploys every push to main.

## Safety

- The crisis guard is a regex in `src/lib/hermen/crisis.ts`. It runs in the browser
  (the crisis panel shows at once) and on the server (it adds the safety rule to the prompt).
- Chats are never stored. Only cards the user chooses to save, keyed to their
  pseudonymous Masky `sub`.
